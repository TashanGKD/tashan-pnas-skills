# PNAS Prose Guardrails

## Register

- Use clear, compact scientific English.
- Prefer concrete subjects and active verbs when the actor matters.
- Use passive voice when the procedure or result matters more than the actor.
- Avoid hype, slogans, and vague impact language.
- Avoid long noun stacks when a clause is clearer.
- Define specialized terms before relying on them.

## Claim Strength

- `demonstrate` or `show`: direct and sufficient evidence.
- `reveal`: use sparingly for clear mechanistic insight.
- `indicate` or `suggest`: partial or indirect support.
- `are consistent with`: compatibility, not proof.
- `may` or `could`: hypothesis or implication.

## Common Repairs

- Replace chronology with logic.
- Move the main claim to the first sentence of a Results paragraph.
- Split mixed Results and Discussion paragraphs.
- Replace "important" with the specific reason it matters.
- Replace "novel" with the precise difference from prior work, if verified.
- Replace "universal" with the tested parameter or system range.

## Citation Flags

Flag statements that require citation:

- historical priority
- consensus claims
- parameter ranges
- experimental facts outside the user's data
- claims about PNAS, journal policy, or field standards
- novelty or "first demonstration" claims
