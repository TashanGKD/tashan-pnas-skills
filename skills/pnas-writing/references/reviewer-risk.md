# PNAS Reviewer Risk Audit

Check these before calling a section PNAS-ready.

## Significance Risk

- Is the significance meaningful outside the immediate method or model?
- Does the text explain why the question matters before technical details?
- Does the paper overclaim broad impact from narrow evidence?

## Evidence Risk

- Is every major claim tied to a figure, equation, simulation, experiment, or source?
- Are single-seed simulations separated from ensemble conclusions?
- Are parameter ranges and units defined?
- Are controls, baselines, or limiting cases present?

## Model-To-Biology Risk

- Are model assumptions explicit?
- Does biological language exceed what the model can support?
- Are alternative mechanisms acknowledged?
- Are experimental predictions or validation routes stated when appropriate?

## Writing Risk

- Are Results and Discussion mixed?
- Is the Introduction a literature list rather than a problem-to-gap argument?
- Is the Significance Statement just a compressed abstract?
- Are terms understandable to a broad scientific audience?
